Most furniture mistakes happen before the first cut

📐

Math on scrap paper

Adding up dimensions by hand, forgetting the blade kerf, and finding out the shelf is 3 mm short when it is already cut.

🪵

Wasted sheets

Parts placed by eye on a sheet of plywood leave large offcuts. One extra sheet on a project can cost more than the hardware.

🔩

Assembly in the wrong order

A back panel glued too early, a dado on the wrong face, a missing hole. Fixing order mistakes means taking it all apart.

How it works

From idea to finished piece in four steps

  1. 1

    Describe your project

    Enter part dimensions, or start from a template like a bookshelf, cabinet or desk. Set material thickness, grain direction and joinery.

  2. 2

    Add your stock

    Tell FairyHooks which sheets and boards you have or plan to buy, including offcuts already in the shop.

  3. 3

    Get the cut plan

    The optimizer nests every part, accounts for kerf and edge trim, and orders the cuts so each rip and crosscut is safe and repeatable.

  4. 4

    Follow the assembly guide

    Labeled parts, a hardware list and illustrated steps in the right order. Print it or keep it open on a tablet at the bench.

Features

Built for the way woodworkers actually work

Cut list optimizer

Nests parts across sheet goods and boards to use as few sheets as possible, with utilization reported for each one.

Kerf and trim compensation

Set your blade width and factory edge trim once. Every dimension in the plan already includes them.

Grain direction control

Lock parts to the grain so doors and visible panels match, or allow rotation for hidden parts to save material.

Cutting sequence

Rips first, then crosscuts, with each cut numbered. Ready for a table saw, track saw or panel saw.

Assembly instructions

Step-by-step order, part labels that match the cut list, and a hardware and fastener checklist.

Offcut inventory

Save usable offcuts and let the next project use them before opening a new sheet.

Metric and imperial

Work in millimeters or inches with fractions. Switch any time without retyping dimensions.

Export and print

Export the cut list as CSV, print labeled layouts, or share a plan link with a client or a shop.

FAQ

Questions

What materials does FairyHooks support?

Sheet goods such as plywood, MDF, particle board and melamine, plus dimensional lumber and hardwood boards. You set the size and thickness of every stock item.

How does it handle the saw blade kerf?

You enter your blade width once. The optimizer leaves that gap between every part, so finished parts come out at the exact size in the plan.

Can I keep wood grain consistent?

Yes. By default parts keep their orientation along the grain. You can allow rotation for parts that will not be seen to save material.

Do I need to install anything?

No. FairyHooks runs in the browser on desktop, tablet and phone. The free planner on this page works offline once loaded.

Does it work with CNC machines?

CSV export is available today. Direct DXF and SVG export for CNC is on the roadmap. Tell us your machine when you request early access.
